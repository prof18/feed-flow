# Article notifications

FeedFlow checks RSS feeds in the background. Article notifications use the existing refresh work, so delivery timing remains controlled by the operating system. This is background RSS checking, not instant push delivery from YouTube. The feature reuses stored feed item titles, source titles, image URLs, and feed notification settings; it does not scrape pages or call the YouTube API.

## Modes and feed settings

The global notification mode is one of **By feed** (`FEED_SOURCE`), **By category** (`CATEGORY`), **All combined** (`GROUPED`), or **Per article** (`ARTICLE`). The default remains **By feed**, and stored mode values are preserved. Per-article mode still honors each feed's notification enabled setting. Changing the global mode does not change feed settings. The other three modes retain their existing summary notification behavior.

## Per-article batch

A background refresh selects pending articles from notification-enabled feeds, excluding items already marked as notified, read, or blocked by current eligibility rules. An article read before the refresh that would announce it is never notified. Turning notifications on for a feed (per feed, for all feeds, or in the feed's settings) marks the articles it already has as notified, and a feed's first successful fetch marks its back-catalog as notified, so only articles that arrive later are announced. "First fetch" means the feed has no `last_sync_timestamp` yet; local fetches and GReader/Feedbin account syncs stamp every subscribed feed after a successful sync, including feeds that returned no articles. Articles a sync account reports as read are marked notified too. This applies to every notification mode. Every pending article gets its own notification, newest first, with no "N more" summary. On Android a batch can show only as many as the tray holds (see below); the older ones beyond that are marked notified, so they cannot push newer notifications out on a later refresh.

Each article notification uses the trimmed article title, falling back to the existing new-articles title if it is blank, and the feed's source title as its body. It uses the stored image URL when available. Images must be valid HTTP(S) URLs. Each platform allows up to one second per image and five seconds of image preparation per batch; after the budget is used, subsequent notifications are sent without images. These timers bound requested image work; native decoding and cancellation cleanup are cooperative, so they are not hard real-time deadlines. Android asks Coil for a bitmap bounded to 1024 pixels. iOS requests a Nuke thumbnail bounded to 1024 pixels, encodes it as JPEG at quality 0.8, and omits attachments larger than 2 MiB. Invalid, missing, failed, or timed-out images fall back to text. The iOS attachment helper removes staged files after submission and prunes its own orphan files older than 24 hours when it starts.

Article notifications use stable identifiers derived from the full item ID. Repeating a submission before acknowledgement updates the same platform notification request/key rather than creating a second entry. The sender acknowledges each article ID only after native submission succeeds. Acknowledgement updates notification-pending state, not read state. Android keeps its active notifications under a conservative 40-key soft limit. The platform constant is `MAX_PACKAGE_NOTIFICATIONS = 50`, and at that point the framework refuses the *new* notification and keeps the older ones ("Package has already posted or enqueued N notifications. Not showing more."), so an untouched full tray would block every later article indefinitely. When the newest articles do not fit, the oldest article notifications are cancelled to make room; their articles stay unread in the timeline, so nothing is lost from the app itself. Android frees a cancelled slot asynchronously, so a batch does not count the notifications it just evicted against its capacity. Notifications other than new-article ones, and article notifications belonging to the batch being posted, are never evicted; when the tray is full of other notifications there is nothing to evict and the articles stay pending for a later refresh. These rules reduce duplicates and avoid acknowledging known failed submissions, but a process crash between OS submission and database acknowledgement can still cause a retry. Submission success does not guarantee visible delivery.

The first successful submission in a batch requests the existing sound; later submissions in that batch are silent. Permission, channel settings, Focus, OS scheduling, tray limits, and platform presentation remain controlled by the operating system. FeedFlow posts no group summary of its own: Android groups an app's notifications by itself once there are several, so individual payloads do not guarantee physically separate presentation.

## Removing notifications once read

Every new-article notification carries a tag naming whose read state retires it: `article-<item id>`, `feed-source-<feed id>`, `category-<category id>` or `uncategorized`, or `grouped` (`NotificationTag`). Android stores it as the notification tag; iOS stores it in the user info under `tag`, because the iOS identifier has to stay unique per post.

`ReadNotificationResolver` decides which of a list of tags have nothing unread behind them: an article tag once that article is read; a feed tag once that feed has no unread articles; a category, `uncategorized` or `grouped` tag once the feeds it covers that send notifications have none. Feeds with notifications turned off are left out of those last three, so an old unread article in a silent feed cannot keep them around, but turning a feed's notifications off retires nothing by itself: its own article and feed notifications stay until they are read. Tags the resolver does not recognise are never retired, and notifications without a tag, as older versions posted them, are left alone.

Each platform watches the same change signal, a never-executed query on the articles table, lists its tray, asks the resolver, and removes what comes back. On Android `FeedFlowApp` collects the signal for the life of the process and `AndroidNotifier.dismissReadNotifications` cancels by tag. On iOS `ReadNotificationWatcher` starts with the app; the live-query gate pauses the signal in the background, so it works while the app is active and catches up, read state that arrived through sync included, when it is opened again. The resolver's reads run inside `withSuspensionGuard`, because a read still open when iOS suspends the app gets it killed (0xdead10cc), and `staleTags` is `@Throws(Exception::class)`, so a failure reaches Swift instead of terminating the app.

## Taps and opening articles

Tapping an article opens FeedFlow with a link containing that article's encoded item ID. The app then uses the configured article-opening behavior, including feed-specific overrides. URL-less items open from their feed-provided content. If reader mode cannot handle a URL, including YouTube links, existing opening rules fall back to the configured browser. The notification work does not make a new YouTube lookup.

## Verification

Focused automated tests check the shared query and exact-ID acknowledgement; native payloads, image fallback, capacity or submission failures, and sound selection. For example:

```sh
./gradlew --quiet --console=plain :shared:jvmTest --tests 'com.prof18.feedflow.shared.domain.notification.*'
./gradlew --quiet --console=plain :androidApp:testGooglePlayDebugUnitTest --tests 'com.prof18.feedflow.android.notifications.*'
(cd iosApp && ./.scripts/generate-project.sh)
xcodebuild -project iosApp/FeedFlow.xcodeproj -scheme NotificationTests -destination "$SIMULATOR_DESTINATION" test -quiet
```

Set `SIMULATOR_DESTINATION` to an available simulator, for example `platform=iOS Simulator,name=iPhone 17 Pro`. If that simulator is absent, choose an available leased simulator and use its exact Xcode destination; the current implementation validation uses iPhone 17 as the approved substitute. The standalone `NotificationTests` scheme covers the native sender, image attachment loader, queued deep-link store, and the read-notification watcher. The read rule is covered by `ReadNotificationResolverTest` against a real database. Shared Android host coverage for worker integration is in `FeedDownloadWorkerTest` and runs with `./gradlew --quiet --console=plain :shared:testAndroidHostTest`.

Native sender tests do not exercise full app routing or visible OS presentation. The named Android and iOS Maestro notification and deep-link flows in the [E2E catalog](../e2e/maestro/maestro-e2e-tests.md) cover app-level routing and settings behavior. Human validation on a phone is still needed to confirm actual OS presentation, image display, sound, warm and cold taps, and browser or YouTube handoff on the user's installed setup. Record the device and OS and treat background timing as OS-controlled.

The repository gate is `./gradlew --quiet --console=plain detekt allTests`, plus the Android and Desktop builds, iOS formatting, iOS `FeedFlow` build, and the named Maestro flows described in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md). The standalone notification test command above supplements that gate. Passing a sender test or gate does not establish that the OS displayed a notification.
